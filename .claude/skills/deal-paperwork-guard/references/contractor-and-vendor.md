# Contractor and vendor work

Covers someone doing work for Malik (a builder, editor, designer, tech) and Malik doing work for
someone else. Lower stakes than a partnership, but this is where the IP quietly goes missing.

## When Malik is hiring

- **Scope, deliverables, and revisions.** What exactly is being delivered, in what format, how many
  revision rounds are included. Unbounded revisions are how a fixed fee becomes unpaid labor.
- **Rate and payment schedule.** Fixed fee or hourly, deposit up front, milestones, and net terms.
  Never pay 100% up front.
- **IP assignment — the one people forget.** In the US, a contractor owns what they create unless the
  contract assigns it in writing. "Work made for hire" language alone is not enough for most
  deliverables; include a present-tense assignment ("Contractor hereby assigns all right, title and
  interest to Client"). Without it, Malik can pay in full for a logo, a build, or an edit and not own it.
- **Source files.** Say the deliverable includes editable source files, not just exports.
- **Confidentiality and non-solicit** of Malik's clients.
- **Termination / kill fee.** How either side ends it and what's owed for work completed.
- **Classification.** A contractor sets their own hours and methods. If Malik controls how and when
  they work, they may legally be an employee — a real tax exposure, not a technicality.

## When Malik is the vendor

- **Payment protection.** Deposit up front (50% is normal for event and creative work), balance due
  before or on delivery. Late fee stated.
- **Scope guard.** Define what's included, and say that anything outside it is a change order at a
  stated rate. This is how "one small extra thing" stops being free.
- **Credit clause.** Malik retains the right to be credited and to use the work in his portfolio.
  See `../assets/credit-and-attribution-clause.md`.
- **Licensing, not transfer.** Consider licensing usage rather than assigning ownership, unless the
  price reflects a full buyout.
- **Cancellation and rescheduling terms**, and what he keeps if the client cancels.
- **Limitation of liability** capped at fees paid, and no personal guarantee.

## Red flag

Starting work before a signed scope and deposit. Once the work is delivered, Malik has no leverage —
only an invoice and hope. If work must start immediately, a signed email confirming scope, price, and
payment terms is enforceable and takes five minutes. There is no situation too urgent for that.
