# Red flags

Check any partnership or collaboration against this list. These are the patterns that preceded the
HESS Club and All Access Art Show endings. One of these is a conversation; several together mean
stop and paper it before another hour of work goes in.

## Structural

- **No document exists** and the relationship is described as "we trust each other."
- **Work is already underway** with terms unsettled — leverage is gone the moment it's delivered.
- **The split is stated but not what it's a split of** — "50/50" means nothing without gross vs. net
  and who defines the expenses.
- **Contribution is asymmetric but the split isn't**, with no performance condition. This is the art
  show, precisely.
- **No exit clause.** If the only way out is to quit and abandon the business, that's not a
  partnership, it's a hostage situation with better branding.
- **The other party controls the money, the accounts, or the client relationships**, and Malik
  controls the work.
- **The brand or business name is in someone else's control** — domain, LLC filing, social handles,
  Google Business listing.

## Behavioral

- **Credit is being taken publicly that doesn't match the work** — posts, intros, bios, "we built."
  Watch this early. It is the leading indicator, and it showed up before both prior endings.
- **"We'll figure that out later"** in response to a direct question about money, ownership, or credit.
  A good partner answers the question; deferring it is the answer.
- **Resistance to writing it down**, especially framed as "don't you trust me?" Reversal of the burden.
  Trustworthy people sign things. The document protects them too.
- **Enthusiasm without follow-through** — big talk in meetings, missed deliverables after. Compare
  what was promised three weeks ago to what shipped.
- **The relationship's terms keep drifting** in one direction across conversations.
- **Malik is doing the unglamorous work** (setup, ops, client management, cleanup) while the other
  person is doing the visible work (talking, posting, being seen).

## The gut check

Ask Malik directly: *"Does this feel like the art show did in year two?"* He knows the feeling better
than any checklist can describe it. If the answer is anything but a clean no, slow the deal down and
get it papered before more work goes in. Trusting his read of the pattern is the point — the
paperwork is just how the read gets enforced.

## What to do with a red flag

Name it once, specifically, without accusing anyone of bad intent — most of these come from
optimism, not malice. Then propose the concrete fix: the clause, the term sheet, the conversation.
The finding is only useful attached to a remedy.
