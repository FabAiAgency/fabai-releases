# Credit and attribution — drop-in language

This is the clause that failed Malik twice. Both times it was assumed rather than written, and both
times someone else's name ended up on work Malik carried. Include a version of it in every
partnership, collaboration, contractor, and client agreement.

Pick the version that fits, fill in the brackets, and adapt the wording to the document's voice.

---

## For a partnership or collaboration

> **Credit and attribution.** [Name] is credited as [Founder / Creator / Producer] of [Venture] in
> all public materials, including but not limited to websites, social media, press, printed
> materials, event signage, award submissions, and pitch or investor materials. Neither party may
> represent the work of the venture as solely their own, or describe the other's contribution
> inaccurately, in any public or client-facing context. Public announcements naming either party's
> role require the other's prior approval. This obligation survives the end of this agreement:
> after either party leaves, each retains the right to accurately describe their role and to
> display the work in their portfolio, and neither may claim the other's contributions as their own.

## For contractor work Malik performs

> **Credit and portfolio rights.** Provider is credited as [role] wherever the work is publicly
> displayed or distributed, in the form "[credit line]," unless the parties agree otherwise in
> writing. Provider retains the right to display the work in his portfolio, reels, website, and
> marketing materials, and to accurately describe his role in it, beginning [on delivery / on public
> release / on (date)].

## For contractors Malik hires

> **Attribution.** Contractor may not publicly represent Client's projects, clients, or brand as
> Contractor's own work or as a partnership between the parties. Contractor may list the engagement
> in a portfolio, accurately described as work performed for Client, [with Client's prior written
> approval / after public release of the project].

## For sponsors and clients

> **Producer credit.** [Venture / Provider] is identified as producer of the [event / activation] in
> event materials, signage, and any post-event recap or press. Client's use of images and footage
> from the [event / activation] includes attribution to [credit line] where the format allows.

---

## Why to insist, and how to say it

If pushback comes, the honest framing is that credit terms protect *both* people. They prevent the
argument two years later when memories have drifted and each person genuinely remembers it
differently. Nobody is being accused of anything by writing down who did what.

The thing to watch for: someone who is happy to sign the money terms but resistant to the credit
terms is telling you exactly what they intend to take.
