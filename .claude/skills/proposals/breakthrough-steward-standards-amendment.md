# Proposed amendment to `breakthrough-steward` — admit STANDARDS, not just methods

**Status:** DRAFT for Malik's call. Not applied. The steward's own doctrine says
*extend before create*, so this is written as an amendment to the existing skill rather
than as a second noticing seat.

**Why this exists:** on 2026-09-02 Malik asked for a skill that catches "philosophical
findings that are skill worthy when you and I are working." A separate `skill-worthy-radar`
skill was drafted before the existing stack was checked — that was the wrong move, and it was
deleted. `breakthrough-steward` already IS the noticing function, and it is stronger than the
draft was (write-then-tell, ruled 2026-08-16, versus asking permission — the exact flow the
steward had already tried and rejected for suppressing capture).

But there is a real gap, and it is the one the same conversation exposed.

## The gap

The steward's bar is: *a repeatable **method**, proven at least once in real work, that changed
an outcome — cheaper, faster, or truer.* Its exclusions send preferences to memory.

The paperwork rule that produced `deal-paperwork-guard` fails that bar on every clause and is
still obviously skill-worthy:

- It was not proven in a task. It was drawn from **losing two businesses** — the HESS Club role
  and eight years of the All Access Art Show.
- It made nothing cheaper or faster. It makes things **slower on purpose**, which is the point.
- It is not a preference. It is a standing instruction to **interrupt Malik and push back**.

So a whole admissible class currently falls between the steward's bar and memory: rules born
from lived pattern rather than from a task. Those are the ones Malik most wants held, because
they are the ones that cost him years.

## The proposed amendment

Add a second admissible class alongside the breakthrough, with its own bar.

> **A STANDARD** is a stance Malik intends to be held to, drawn from a pattern in his own
> history or a decision he has now made once and for all. Where a breakthrough answers *how do
> we do this faster*, a standard answers *what do we refuse, and what must be raised even when
> nobody asks*.
>
> **The bar:** it generalizes to situations that have not happened yet; it carries judgment
> rather than fact (facts are memory's); and Malik would be annoyed by silence — the test is
> *if this recurred in six months and the session said nothing, would that be a failure?*
>
> **The signals**, alongside the existing eight: standing-instruction language ("from now on,"
> "never let me," "before any," "I want you to push back on," "make this a standard"); a lesson
> he draws out loud from his own history; the same correction given twice; a reflective
> conversation that resolves into a decision rule.
>
> **Where it does NOT apply:** a reflection that stays contemplative. He reads scripture as a
> living text and applies it to business; when that produces a boundary or a test, capture it,
> and when it stays contemplative, leave it alone. Turning every reflection into a deliverable
> is its own way of missing the point.
>
> **Write-then-tell still governs** — with one carve-out already in the doctrine that bites
> here: a standard changing how money moves, how a client is contacted, or what may be sent
> needs a ruling. `deal-paperwork-guard` gates deals, so it was built at Malik's explicit
> instruction rather than self-ratified. Standards of that kind get written and then explicitly
> put to him, not written and mentioned in passing.
>
> **One honesty clause at creation:** a standard stated in a raw moment — right after a
> betrayal, or in the flush of a breakthrough — is worth capturing AND worth saying plainly
> that it can be revised once it has been lived with. A skill is a commitment he can change,
> not a vow.
